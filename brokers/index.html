Why this review

Bring operating questions into the preparation process.

Pricing exceptions, unbilled freight and critical work held by one person can become difficult questions during diligence. We examine agreed records and workflows, document what the evidence supports, and identify what needs clarification or specialist review.

What's included

Three parts, one report.

Scope, records and limits are confirmed in writing before any work begins.

01

Margin leakage and operating controls

Use a defined period of invoice, freight and credit records to examine agreed pricing exceptions, freight recovery and recurring adjustment patterns. Findings show the source records, calculation, assumptions and suggested corrective action. Rebate and special-pricing claim gaps are noted where records show them; recovery work is scoped separately.

02

Transition and continuity risks

Review owner and key-person dependencies, customer and supplier concentration, and receivables-aging warning signs where supporting records are available. Identify missing evidence, role ownership and questions to resolve before handoff.

03

Workflow and capacity opportunities

Identify selected bottlenecks, repeated manual work and opportunities to make processes more transferable. Estimated hours and operating capacity are presented separately from margin dollars.

The report

A report the seller can act on.

  • Executive summary of priorities and unresolved questions.
  • Findings linked to the records reviewed, with confidence and limitations.
  • Corrective actions with suggested owners.
  • A prioritized 90-day transition and improvement plan.
  • A review call to discuss findings and next steps.

FAQ

Scope questions.

Does this replace the accountant or attorney?

No. The review complements financial and legal diligence. It does not provide a quality-of-earnings opinion, valuation, tax advice, add-back acceptance, legal opinion or physical inventory count.

Who commissions the work?

A seller can engage us for readiness work through a broker introduction. Buyer-side reviews are separately engaged and scoped.

Who receives the report?

Report recipients and permitted sharing are agreed in the engagement.

Will every issue have a dollar value?

Only where the records support it. Missing evidence, projected improvements and capacity estimates are labeled.
